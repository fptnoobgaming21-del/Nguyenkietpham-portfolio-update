The design challenge

How could a takeaway croissant package protect its contents, invite people to open it, and make breakfast feel playful? BUY LES explores that question through the visual language of a neighborhood French café.

Who it is for

The intended audience is café customers picking up a croissant to go. This is a design intention, not a customer segment validated through interviews.

My role

I developed the packaging concept, explored structures, created the graphic system and dieline artwork, and assembled printed prototypes. The project follows the design from early sketches through the final presentation.

DESIGN DECISIONS

One café. One visual language.

A focused palette

Red gives the awning and croissant a shared identity. White keeps the panels open, while black outlines make the storefront illustration easy to distinguish.

BONJOUR!

Type with a friendly voice

Rounded, bold display lettering gives the greetings a playful tone. Smaller supporting copy takes a quieter role on the information panels.

A structure that tells a story

Striped panels read as a café awning, while a repeated croissant shape connects the package silhouette to the product inside.

OUTCOME & REFLECTION

A coherent system.
An honest next step.

What the project demonstrates

The outcome is a café-inspired packaging system supported by sketches, vector artwork and printed prototypes. The same shapes, colors and illustration style connect the front, back, sides and opening.

What changed

The documented direction moved from a yellow character-based carrier to a red-and-white café concept. Later versions added a more pronounced awning, an opening cue and dedicated product-information panels. The files show these revisions, but do not document formal user feedback or usability testing.

What I would test next

Observe first-time opening, check how a real croissant fits, and evaluate carrying comfort and fold durability. Food-contact materials, print tolerances and production costs also need validation before manufacture.

What remains exploratory

The early brief considered a bistro-table experience and reuse after eating. The final documentation demonstrates the café-shaped box; a reusable table-and-chair function is not established.
